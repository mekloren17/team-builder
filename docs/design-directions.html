A new setting for the cards we already have

The player cards stay exactly as they are. This page looks at what Italian amateur players find attractive and proposes three new environments for everything around the cards, each shown on a PC browser and on a phone.

Short answer: the cards are glossy, rounded and metallic, while the current pink-newspaper style is flat and square, so they look stuck on. The environment that suits them best is Floodlights (direction A): a dark night-pitch setting, the way the sports centre looks at 21:00, where gold, silver and bronze cards shine like they do in a game menu. It also gives the two teams their natural colours: the yellow and orange bibs.
1 · The audience

What this crowd finds attractive

Desk research plus knowledge of the culture, not a survey. There are no official figures on amateur calcetto; the real test is showing these screens to the group (last section).

Calcetto is a ritual among friends

Most amateur 5-a-side is played by fixed groups of friends or colleagues booking the same pitch every week. The ritual is concrete: the WhatsApp poll, the 21:00 slot, artificial turf under floodlights, yellow and orange bibs, the beer after.

So: the materials of that evening feel personal in a way a generic sports app never does.

Italians love a mark out of ten

The Monday pagelle and the voti of Fantacalcio made rating players a national habit.

So: the overall on the cards is already the star. The environment should frame the numbers, not compete with them.

Collectible cards are shared childhood

Football stickers have been around in Italy since the early 1960s ("ce l'ho, ce l'ho, mi manca"), and the video-game card is today's version. Everyone already reads "face, number, name" without thinking.

So: keeping the cards is right. They need a setting that treats them as the valuable objects they are.

1990s Serie A is peak nostalgia

Clubs and brands keep reissuing that era, like Kappa's 90s-style Fiorentina shirt for 2025/26. Retro shirts are worn as fashion.

So: loud shirt patterns and italic lettering read as cool, but they get busy next to colourful cards.

Young fans read football on social media

Cronache di Spogliatoio built its look from a player photo and big uppercase text in yellow and white. The Kings League uses the language of video games.

So: few colours, very big type, dark grounds that make photos and cards pop. Screens should look good as a screenshot in a chat.

It's a website, used on PCs too

People open it from a WhatsApp link on the phone, but some use it on a computer, and admins build the teams there. Today the app is a single 672px column even on a wide screen.

So: every direction needs a real desktop layout: navigation at the top, the match beside the squad, cards in a grid.

2 · Where we are

The cards in today's "Le pagelle" setting

  • Works: big condensed numbers, strong contrast, nothing like the usual green-and-grey sports app.
  • Clash: the system bans rounded corners, gradients and shadows, and the cards are made of exactly those. They look pasted onto a newspaper.
  • Mood: a newspaper is something you read the day after. Signing up and seeing the teams should feel like the evening itself.
  • On a PC: one narrow column with a lot of empty pink on both sides; cards only fit three or four per row.
  • Pink on a monitor: large areas of pale pink read as faded on a bright desktop screen, more than on a phone.
3 · Direction A · recommended

Floodlights

The 21:00 pitch

The site looks like the sports centre on a Thursday night: dark artificial turf with mowing stripes, a floodlight glow at the top, a scoreboard with lit dot digits for the sign-up count and the win chance. The main button is the fluorescent yellow of a bib, and the two teams are always yellow and orange.

Dark grounds are where metallic cards look best: gold glows, silver reads as silver, the card shadows finally make sense. On a PC it looks like a broadcast graphic rather than an admin panel.

Colours
Type
Tall stadium-sign lettering (Big Shoulders Display), a dot-matrix face for scoreboard numbers (Doto), a plain sans for text.
Signature
The counter flicking from 06 to 07 when you sign up.
Risks
Dark screens suffer more from sun glare on a phone; keep text pure white and large. Fluorescent yellow works only on dark, never as text on white.
4 · Direction B

The Album

Sticker album page

The site is an album: a blue cover band with a yellow edge, a lightly dotted page, and each match is a spread with slots. The cards stay as they are and sit in the slots like stickers already stuck in; the empty dashed slots show how many players are missing.

The most nostalgic and the brightest, so the best in sunlight. The cards' glossy look fits a sticker better than a newspaper, though less than a dark ground.

Colours
Type
A heavy block face (Archivo Black) for headlines and numbers, a narrow face for text.
Signature
Your card dropping into its slot with a tiny bounce.
Risks
Silver cards are pale on a pale page. Keep it generic: no sticker-publisher logo or "Calciatori" wording.
5 · Direction C

The Kit Room

90s shirts

A loud geometric 90s shirt pattern only in the header band, then a solid deep plum page with gold trim and sporty italic lettering. Each group could pick its own "kit" pattern and colours, which suits multiple groups well.

The most playful. To leave room for the cards, the pattern never goes behind content.

Colours
Type
A heavy italic sports face (Kanit) for headlines and numbers.
Signature
The group's own kit pattern across the top.
Risks
Gold trim competes with gold cards. Colours can accidentally recall a real club. Goes out of fashion fastest.

7 · Built for the PC too

A desktop layout, whatever style wins

  • Wider page from 1024px: about 1100px instead of the current 672px column.
  • Two columns on the group home: the next match and the "I'm in" button on the left, the cards of who's in on the right, with empty slots for open spots.
  • Navigation at the top on a PC, the tab bar at the bottom on a phone (this already happens from 768px).
  • Team proposals side by side: the two teams in two columns of cards, so admins can compare and move players on a big screen.
  • Hover states on cards and rows, since PC users have a mouse.
8 · Recommendation

Go with Floodlights

It is the only setting where the existing cards look like they belong: glossy metal on a dark ground. It is the most specific to how calcetto is really played, works best on a PC, and is the lightest switch, mostly colours and fonts. To deal with sunlight on phones, keep all text pure white and large, and keep the main actions in bib yellow, which stays visible in glare.

If the group prefers a light site, the Album is the runner-up.
